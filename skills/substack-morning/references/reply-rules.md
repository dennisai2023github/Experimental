# Reply Rules for the Morning Brief

These rules govern every Reply A and Reply B in the brief. They sit on top of Dennis's `writing-style` and `dennis-voice` skills and `references/anti-fabrication.md`. Where this file is more specific, it wins for replies.

## What a good comment does

A comment on someone else's post or Note is how readers who do not know Dennis discover him. It works when it shows real thinking about *their* piece. A comment that could be pasted under any post on the topic does nothing for him and slightly annoys the writer.

So every reply responds to one specific thing the writer said, by quoting a short phrase or naming the claim, and then adds one thing the writer did not say.

## Reply A: value added, ready to paste

Reply A must add value on its own. Pick the single strongest of these moves for the item:

1. **Apply one of Dennis's stated positions** from `references/pov.md` to their argument. Example: their post treats a regulation as the target; Dennis's position is that "regulation is the floor", so the reply pushes on what sits above the floor. Present it as his view ("I'd put it differently: ...", "The way I see it ..."), because these positions are on record as his.
2. **Name a gap or tension** in their argument and ask the question that exposes it. The question must be one only this post could prompt.
3. **Connect to a framework or regulation**, with an accurate, checkable reference: EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, DORA, NIS2, GDPR, SEC cyber disclosure rules, and similar. Cite only what you are confident is correct; if unsure of an article number or clause, name the instrument without the clause.
4. **Extend their point one step** into practice: what it means for a board, a CISO, a risk owner, a governance body.

Governance18 components can be named when they genuinely fit ("this is really a vendor and third-party management problem"), but Reply A is not an ad. Use the model's language as a lens, not as a plug.

Reply A must never contain:
- An experience, client story, number, date, or person from Dennis's life that is not in `pov.md` (see `anti-fabrication.md`). "In my work with banks..." is invented unless he wrote it there.
- Self-promotion: no links, no "I wrote about this", no AISGRC mention, no newsletter mention.
- Generic praise openers ("Great post", "Love this", "So true").
- Tips or statistics with no source.

## Reply B: same value, plus Dennis's own view

Reply B starts from Reply A and adds exactly one slot where Dennis's own opinion or experience would make the reply stronger than anything the AI can supply. Write the slot as a bracketed prompt that tells him precisely what to type:

- Good: `[one example where a board you advised accepted "the vendor" as the accountable owner]`
- Good: `[your one-line verdict: is 70% coverage acceptable at Day 90?]`
- Bad: `[your thoughts]`, `[add experience here]`

Rules for B:
- Exactly one slot is the default. Use two only when the reply genuinely needs two separate inputs.
- The sentence around the slot must read naturally once he fills it in.
- B may be up to 15 words longer than A to make room for the slot.
- Keep the same quoted hook and the same core point as A, so he can choose either without reading twice.

## Shape and length

- 20 to 80 words for Reply A. One point per reply.
- Notes replies run shorter (20 to 50 words). Article comments can use the full range.
- First person, peer to peer, practitioner to practitioner. No lecturing.
- Plain verbs. Varied sentence length. Some messiness is fine.
- End on a question only when it is a real question the writer could answer. Otherwise end on the point.

## Banned (from Dennis's voice rules and SYSTEM.md)

- Em dashes and en dashes anywhere, and `--` as a substitute. Use a period, comma, colon, or semicolon. The render step blocks the brief if any slip through, so fix them while drafting.
- Self-answered rhetorical questions ("The result? ...").
- Tricolon lists used for emphasis.
- Punchy fragment runs.
- Filler transitions: "it is worth noting", "importantly", "at the end of the day".
- AI vocabulary: delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, foster, harness, streamline, robust, seamless, landscape, tapestry, journey (as metaphor), navigate (as metaphor), game-changer, transformative.
- Unnamed authority: "studies show", "experts agree".

Note that `pov.md` itself contains em dashes because it is Dennis's source document. Never copy them into a reply.

## Summary and "why" lines

Each item also gets:
- `summary`: two sentences on what the post or Note argues, in plain words. No judgment.
- `why`: one line on why it made today's brief, naming the real signal (for example "Writer you follow · 3 comments so far · on agentic AI accountability").

Both follow the same banned list.

## Self-check before render

For every item, confirm:
1. The reply quotes or names something specific from this post.
2. It adds one thing the post does not say.
3. Every fact, position, or reference can be pointed to: the post, `pov.md`, or a public instrument named accurately.
4. No em dash, en dash, or `--`.
5. Reply B has a precise bracketed slot, and reads well once filled.
